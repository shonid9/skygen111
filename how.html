THE PIPELINE

Four stages. No shortcuts.

Scroll. Each stage stacks on the last, the way evidence should.

01

Understand the task

What is the user asking to verify, and what would count as evidence? A receipt and a contract don’t get the same plan. Neither does a screenshot of either.

02

Parse the material

Entities, dates, amounts, claims, references, document properties and relationships. A story becomes a list. Lists can be checked.

03

Build a source plan

Select the relevant connected systems, public sources and internal checks. Each claim gets the witness that was actually there.

04

Return an evidence map

Confirmed facts, conflicts, suspicious signals, source quality and unresolved gaps. In that order. With links. Without adjectives.

CONFIDENCE IS NOT CERTAINTY

We stop at the evidence. Not at 100.

A trustworthy system says when the evidence is weak. The needle goes as far as the sources allow, and then it stops, on purpose.

What we refuse to invent

Adjectives in the report

“Likely”, “clearly”, “obviously” are not evidence. We use verdicts, sources and links.

A trustworthy system should say when evidence is weak. “Not verified” is often more useful than pretending something is true or false.
